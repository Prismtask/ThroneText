Main Campaign — Design Document v2.0

The Sundering Accord

A five-act storyline woven through dungeon, city, and the bonds you forge

Acts: 5 Cities: 20 Levels 1 → 60+ BBEG: Arch-Lich Valdremoch
Overview
The Shape of the Story

The world did not break all at once. It cracked slowly — a dead caravan here, a missing scout there, a temple's sacred flame snuffed out in the dead of night. The player character begins as no one special: a drifter, a sell-sword, a desperate scholar, a sailor between ships. They arrive in Solmere, the temperate crossroads at the heart of the continent, carrying nothing but their skills and a letter that turns out to be addressed to someone who is already dead.

Over five acts, the player traces a conspiracy that spans every biome, every region, and every faction. At its center is Arch-Lich Valdremoch, a former court mage who did not merely seek immortality — he found it, and in doing so, bargained away the last binding treaty that held the world's supernatural forces in equilibrium. That treaty was called the Sundering Accord. Its destruction is already underway. The player must race to understand what it was, why it mattered, and whether any thread of it can be saved — or whether something entirely new must be forged from the ruins.

The Monster Girls. In the world's broken places, the released powers have left echoes — fragments of the Accord's magic that have taken feminine form. These are the Monster Girls the player captures, befriends, and recruits. They are not mere trophies. Each one carries a shard of the old world's memory. The House where they live is more than storage — it is a sanctuary for broken things. And the Bond formed at 200 affection, sealed with a ring and a wedding accessory, is a tiny, personal re-weaving of the Accord itself.

The Villain
Arch-Lich Valdremoch
Valdremoch
"He Who Unmade His Own Name" — The Sigil-Breaker, First Lich of the Eastern Conclave

Valdremoch was once Aldric Vorn, High Artificer and Court Mage to the old Conclave that governed the East. A century ago he was the chief signatory of the Sundering Accord — a magical treaty that bound four supernatural entities (a Fey Lord, a Primordial Elemental, a Shadow Dragon, and a Vampire Lord) to a set of territorial limits, preventing them from making war on the mortal world.

The Accord demanded a living witness — someone who would maintain its terms, someone whose continued existence was itself a binding clause. Aldric accepted. He then spent decades planning his escape: if he were no longer living, the Accord would dissolve, and the four bound powers would scatter in chaos — chaos he intended to harvest to fuel his own apotheosis into undead immortality.

He is not loud or theatrical. He is patient, methodical, and genuinely believes he has done the world a favor by removing a lie it called peace. He speaks in the measured tones of a professor correcting a student. He is brilliant, entirely without mercy, and has had a century to prepare for the player's interference.

His phylactery — a grimoire sealed with his own petrified heart — is hidden inside the Isle of Glass, locked behind wards written in a language that pre-dates the Accord itself. He does not appear as a random dungeon boss. He is the hidden final superboss, encountered only after the player has proven themselves across four biomes and survived the Pandemonium.

Lore Note — The Four Bound Powers
The Accord's Prisoners & Their Heralds

When Valdremoch dissolved the Accord by achieving undeath, four ancient beings were released from their obligations. They are not automatically hostile — they are simply free. Each one has left a trail across the world, and their Heralds — powerful superbosses that guard the milestone floors of every dungeon — are the echoes of their awakening. The player will encounter these Heralds at Floor 20 and Floor 40 of every city's dungeon:

Sylvana, Queen of Mirrors (Fey Lord) — The Fey Lord herself, imprisoned in the forest biome, now reawakening in Veilholt. She wants her kingdom back and will consume the forest cities to rebuild it. Her superboss form is the Queen of Mirrors Sylvana (Level 21, 380 HP).

Tharuvex the Sunken (Primordial Elemental) — Bound beneath the seafloor off Tidebreak. His reawakening causes storms, shipwrecks, and the rise of Tide Callers. His herald is the Dream-Devouring Slitcurrent (Level 21, 420 HP), a water-abomination that frays reality itself.

The Eternal Shadow (Shadow Dragon) — Its territory was the sky and the dark places between stars. Its herald is Yinglong, the Heaven-Banished Dragon (Level 25, 400 HP), a dragon cast out of the celestial order whose presence blots out the sun.

Vaelthryn, Vampire Lord — Its release triggered the undead surge. Its herald is the Captain of the Everlong Ship (Level 22, 380 HP), an undead pirate who commands a spectral vessel and a crew that cannot die.

Three Additional Heralds — The breaking of the Accord also birthed three other catastrophic echoes: Broodmother Vileheart (Level 20, 350 HP), a corruption of nature's fecundity; Ignis, the Melt-Forge Golem (Level 21, 400 HP), a construct of volcanic rage; and Rientrante, the Administrator (Level 25, 420 HP), a reality-warping entity that flickers between the dungeon's code and the world itself — possibly Valdremoch's own creation.

Act I · Level Cap 10
Act I
The Broken Letter
Cities: Solmere ★ Greyharbor Thornwall Elderfen
Narrative Hook
A Letter for a Dead Man

The player arrives in Solmere carrying a sealed letter from their backstory — a debt, a dying relative's last wish, a job contract, a summons. The recipient, one Orvast Kelm, was a scholar at the Solmere Trade Hall. He died three weeks ago. The innkeeper knows he died strangely: found in the middle of his study, no wounds, every candle in the room burned to a precise equal length.

Inside the letter — which the player is now justified in opening — is a fragment of a magical contract written in two scripts, one of which dissolves as they read it. The surviving half reads: "...the Witness is no longer bound. Seek the roots before the crown falls."

This is Act I's engine: the player doesn't know what the Accord is yet. They only know something is wrong, someone they needed is dead, and the clues point outward from Solmere. The game loop drives the discovery: the player enters the Solmere Dungeon, fights through 10-room floors, and at Floor 20, encounters the first Superboss — a Herald of the broken world.

"Solmere is not exciting, traveler. It is merely the place where every road touches before it goes somewhere more interesting. Stay long enough and everything comes through it — merchants, soldiers, plagues, and prophets. Right now I am very interested in what is coming through it that we cannot yet name." — Maret, Solmere Innkeeper

Why Solmere as the starting city? It is the cartographic and narrative fulcrum of the entire map — a temperate hub with land connections to every major region. It has a temple for divine hooks, a trade hall for political intrigue, a blacksmith for early equipping, an inn that naturally aggregates rumors, and a Guild Hall where the player will eventually raise their level cap. Players of every class and race can find a reason to be here. Its status as the world's marked starting city (★) is earned: it is average enough to orient new players and connected enough to send them anywhere the story needs.

Facility Hook 1–A
What Orvast Knew
The Trade Hall receptionist in Solmere mentions Orvast's locked study. The player can bribe or persuade (CHA check) to gain access. Inside, dungeon-style exploration: a single combat room with Shadow Stalkers, then a reward chest. Loot: Orvast's Journal (key item), 30 gold, Uncommon Robes or Leather Armor.
Reward: Orvast's Journal (key item), unlocks Greyharbor route.
Facility Hook 1–B
The Thornwall Garrison
The road north is plagued by Bandits and Goblin Scouts. The Barracks captain in Thornwall (a city with no trade hall — only shop, inn, and barracks) offers a bounty-style quest: clear 5 floors of the Thornwall Dungeon and recover the stolen supply manifest from the Floor 5 boss. The manifest lists shipments to a buyer in Irondeep.
Reward: Access to Thornwall Barracks merchant, 45 gold, Uncommon Chainmail.
Facility Hook 1–C
The River Town's Grief
In Greyharbor (a river town with Trade Hall and Guild but no port or blacksmith), the recipient of Orvast's unsent letter is a retired cartographer named Besswyn. She has already been visited — by cultists. The player must enter the Greyharbor Dungeon and reach Floor 10 to find the cultist camp. Boss encounter: Temperate Bandit Leader + 2 Cultists.
Reward: Swamp Region Maps (key item), Besswyn joins as a contact, 50 gold.
Dungeon Milestone 1–D
Into the Ashen Mire
Elderfen's Herbalist has been treating an epidemic of "hollow fever" — victims wake up unable to speak, their voices replaced by silence. The swamp north of town is producing Rotten Carcasses and Zombie Minions. The player must clear Floor 20 of the Elderfen Dungeon (swamp biome). First Superboss encounter: one of the seven Heralds appears. The Mire Altar is the boss room at Floor 20. First encounter with Shadow influence.
Reward: Elderfen Herbalist unlocks, Scroll of Fusion (Rare), First Superboss defeated, Act I complete.
Act I Boss
First Superboss Encounter — A Herald Awakens

At Floor 20 of any Act I dungeon, the player stumbles into a Superboss Arena. The specific boss is drawn from the shuffled tier pool of seven Heralds. The first encounter is likely the Broodmother Vileheart (Level 20, 350 HP) if the dungeon is temperate or swamp, or the Captain of the Everlong Ship if the city has a port connection. This is a narrow victory or near-death experience that sets the tone: the world has ancient, terrible things in it, and the player is now strong enough to see them.

Defeating the Herald drops a Legendary-tier item and enough EXP to push the player toward the level cap of 10. The player now has two threads: the cultists pointing east toward Irondeep, and the shadow sickness pointing deeper into the swamp toward Mirefall. The Guild Hall in Solmere or Greyharbor will allow the player to ascend the level cap once they have cleared enough biomes.

Hidden Discovery
The Accord Fragment (Collectible)

In Orvast's study, behind a false shelf, the player finds a wax-sealed cylinder. Inside is one of five fragments of the original Sundering Accord document. Each fragment, when brought to the Arcane Tower in Skylume or the Temple in Coralhaven, contributes to knowledge that can weaken Valdremoch in the final fight. This first fragment is always found; the other four are optional discoveries across Acts II–IV, often dropped by Superbosses or hidden in high-floor dungeon rooms.

Lore Note — Monster Girls in Act I
The Echoes Begin to Appear

As the player dungeons through Solmere, Greyharbor, Thornwall, and Elderfen, they will begin capturing Monster Girls — Fey, Beast, Shadow, and Undead types. These are not random fauna. They are echoes of the Accord's broken clauses: fragments of the four powers that have taken feminine form. A captured Fey girl whispers about "the Queen" (Sylvana). A captured Shadow girl murmurs about "the dark between stars" (Yinglong). The House system, where these girls are stored and recruited, becomes a living archive of the world's damage. The player may not realize it yet, but every Monster Girl recruited is a tiny thread of the Accord being gathered back into the mortal sphere.

Act II · Level Cap 20
Act II
The Open Sea
Cities: Brinewatch Mirefall Saltmarsh Blackwake Tidebreak Coralhaven
Narrative Hook
The Storm That Doesn't Move

Besswyn's maps point to something she calls "the Sunken Marker" — a sea-route waypoint off the southern coast that charts claim is always calm. Sailors at Brinewatch are reporting the opposite: a permanent storm cell has appeared there, capsizing ships, and creatures made of water and lightning are dragging survivors down. The Brinewatch Port and Shipyard are desperate — they need the harbor cleared before any vessel can sail south.

Simultaneously, Mirefall's Black Market has something the player needs: a "tide journal" kept by a smuggler who was the last person to sail through the Sunken Marker before the storm formed. Getting it means dealing with Mirefall's dangerous underground, run by a Lizardfolk crime broker named Ssarak — who is not evil, just mercenary, and may become a recurring ally. The player can access the Black Market in Mirefall, Ashkara, Dunemar, or Blackwake.

Facility Hook 2–A
The Portmaster's Debt
Brinewatch (coastal biome, has Port and Shipyard) needs its harbor cleared. The player must enter the Brinewatch Dungeon and clear Floor 20, where a Coastal Boss guards the sea lanes. The Shipyard then repairs a scout vessel. Introduces sea travel as a mechanic. First encounter with coastal Elementals.
Reward: Sea route unlocked to Saltmarsh and Tidebreak, 80 gold.
Facility Hook 2–B
Ssarak's Price
Mirefall's Black Market broker Ssarak wants three things before he'll hand over the tide journal: a stolen shipment returned (found in the Mirefall Dungeon, Floor 10+), a rival debt collected (fight a Swamp Abomination in the dungeon), and discretion. This is a roleplaying-heavy interaction — the Black Market dialogue system offers bribe, intimidate, or trade options. Violence solves two; a high CHA score solves the third.
Reward: Tide Journal (key item), Ssarak as recurring contact, Rare Elven Mail or Robes.
Facility Hook 2–C
The Pirate's Bargain
Blackwake (sea-caves biome, Port and Black Market only — no shop, no inn) is the only place that knows where the Sunken Marker actually is. Sailing there risks pirate assault (travel event). Once docked, the player must clear the Blackwake Dungeon to Floor 20 to earn the Pirate Council's respect, or fight three consecutive boss floors. The Black Market here sells unique maps.
Reward: Sunken Marker coordinates, Blackwake black market access.
Dungeon Milestone 2–D
Three Harbors of Tidebreak
Tidebreak (coastal, has Port, Shipyard, and Trade Hall) is the southern hub — and it's in crisis. The player must clear Floor 20 of the Tidebreak Dungeon. The boss room reveals something beneath the seafloor pushing upward. First direct evidence of Tharuvex the Sunken awakening. The Floor 20 Superboss here is likely the Dream-Devouring Slitcurrent (Level 21, 420 HP) — a water-abomination that frays reality.
Reward: Tidebreak trade hall restored (opens stock), Epic weapon from shipyard, second Superboss defeated.
Act II Revelation
Coralhaven and the Temple's Warning

After stabilizing Tidebreak, a sea route opens to Coralhaven (tropical island, reachable by sea from Tidebreak, Dunemar, or Isle of Glass). Coralhaven has three Temples, a Herbalist, a Gift Shop, and a Guild Hall — making it a vital sanctuary. Its temples have been receiving shared visions: a figure wrapped in grave-cloth, standing at a lectern, speaking backwards. One temple priest has translated part of it: "I unmade the witness. The four are free. The Glass Island will be last."

This is the first time the player hears of the Isle of Glass as a destination. The Coralhaven Herbalist also has a crucial potion recipe that requires a volcanic ingredient — pointing the player toward Cinderpeak in the East. Act II ends here, with the player knowing two things: the storm at sea is one of the four released powers, and someone deliberately caused all of this. The player should also be approaching or at the Level Cap of 20, ready to ascend at any Guild Hall.

Act II Boss
Second Superboss — Tharuvex's Herald

The Superboss at Tidebreak Floor 20 (or any coastal city's Floor 20) is likely the Dream-Devouring Slitcurrent — Tharuvex's herald. It is not Tharuvex himself; it is the first surge of his waking breath, a herald of water and dream-frayed reality. Defeating it calms the harbor but does not solve the problem; it only pushes Tharuvex back into a half-sleep. The player learns from its death-scream: the name Valdremoch, spoken in the Primordial language of water. Their enemy now has a name.

A second optional Superboss is available at Floor 40 of any coastal city: attempting to reach the Sunken Marker directly will trigger the Captain of the Everlong Ship (Level 22, 380 HP) — the undead pirate vessel that sails between dimensions. This is designed for high-level parties or New Game+ replays. On first playthrough, the storm simply subsides after the Floor 20 Superboss.

Lore Note — Monster Girls in Act II
The Sea Gives Up Its Echoes

Coastal and sea-cave biomes introduce Elemental and Lizardfolk Monster Girls. These echo Tharuvex's influence — they are drawn to water, they dream strangely, and they speak in currents. A recruited Lizardfolk girl from Brinewatch or Mirefall may mention "the Sunken One" in her nightly dialogue. The player can begin forming Bonds (100 affection → engagement ring from the Gift Shop → 200 affection → wedding accessory) with these girls. Each Bond is a tiny thread of the Accord re-knitted — and the wedding accessories, which are soulbound and have Bonded combat effects, represent the player's personal power growing as they heal the world in small ways.

Act III · Level Cap 30
Act III
The Accord's Teeth
Cities: Sunreach Ashkara Dunemar Veilholt Irondeep Cinderpeak
Act III Setup
Splitting the World's Wounds

Act III is the most geographically sprawling act. The player now knows three things: the Accord existed, Valdremoch destroyed it deliberately, and four powers are waking. The Guild Hall requires the player to have cleared multiple unique biomes to their current cap (20 → 30 requires 2 biomes to Floor 30, or 3 biomes to Floor 20, etc.). This naturally pushes the player east and south.

The act sends them into the volcanic-forest region (Veilholt, Cinderpeak) and the desert south (Sunreach, Ashkara, Dunemar) to confront two of the released powers directly — Sylvana in the forest and Vaelthryn's influence in the mountains — while gathering intelligence in the desert cities that will expose Valdremoch's physical location. Both paths eventually converge at Irondeep, the mountain city where the undead surge has taken root. The player cannot avoid Irondeep — but they choose what allies they bring to it.

Facility Hook 3–A
The Savanna Temple's Silence
Sunreach's Temple has gone quiet — the priests stopped sending correspondence. Visiting reveals they have become obsessed with a "voice in the heat haze." The player must clear the Sunreach Dungeon to Floor 20, where an Infernal Commander (Demon boss) guards a buried Accord signing site. The Temple can restore blessings once the site is cleared.
Reward: Accord Fragment 2 (optional), Sunreach temple restored.
Facility Hook 3–B
Ashkara's Black Book
Ashkara's Black Market is the best-connected information network in the south. A broker called "the Glassman" (a Tiefling in tinted spectacles who never gives their real name) has a ledger of payments. Acquiring it requires either an enormous bribe (800+ gold), a theft from the black market vault (clear Ashkara Dungeon Floor 30 and defeat the Clockwork Sentinel vault guard), or trading a rare Monster Girl capture (high CHA check).
Reward: Valdremoch's Agent Ledger (key item, points to Irondeep and Isle of Glass).
Dungeon Milestone 3–C
The Amber Road Ambush
Traveling between Ashkara and Dunemar triggers a travel event: a Dragonkin Ancient Wyrm (Level 30 boss) attacks the caravan. This is not random — it is sent by someone. The Dunemar Trade Hall reveals: a "scholar" matching Valdremoch's description passed through six months ago, purchased three caravans' worth of alchemical components, and left by sea toward an island "not on any map."
Reward: Confirmation of Isle of Glass destination. Epic item dropped by Wyrm.
Dungeon Milestone 3–D
Veilholt is Dreaming
The ancient forest city of Veilholt (has Arcane Tower, Guild Hall, and Herbalist) has sealed its gates. The Arcane Tower is broadcasting a suppression field. The player must clear the Veilholt Dungeon to Floor 40, where the Superboss is Queen of Mirrors Sylvana (Level 21, 380 HP) — one of the Four Bound Powers in her full form. She is the hardest Superboss encountered so far. Her minions, the Sylvana Mirror Copies (minion-only, 1 HP each), exist in enormous numbers and can only be identified from the real Sylvana by a Wisdom check.
Reward: Veilholt Arcane Tower restored, Accord Fragment 3, Sylvana's Mirror Shard (Legendary crafting material).
Act III Main Boss
Vaelthryn's Influence — The Mountain Fortress

Irondeep's mountain fortress (has Blacksmith, Barracks, and Guild Hall) is the act's climax. The player arrives to find the city's barracks running at skeleton staff — literally. Half the garrison has been turned. The undead surge is not random; it is organized. The Barracks captain — the last unturned officer — is the quest giver: she needs the player to clear the Irondeep Dungeon to Floor 40 and end the source.

At Floor 40 of Irondeep, the Superboss is likely the Captain of the Everlong Ship (Level 22, 380 HP) — the undead herald of Vaelthryn, or the Yinglong, Heaven-Banished Dragon (Level 25, 400 HP) if the dungeon RNG draws the Shadow Dragon. Either way, the player confronts one of the Four Bound Powers' direct manifestations. The fight is brutal and requires a full party of 3 allies with learned skills from the Barracks teaching menu.

Vaelthryn
Vampire Lord · One of the Four Bound — Released Signatory of the Sundering Accord

Vaelthryn is not the villain. He is a consequence. The Accord bound him to the mountain biome for a century, and he kept to it — not from goodness, but from calculation. Valdremoch's destruction of the Accord was done without consulting him, and he resents it. He is not fought directly in Act III; his herald (the Everlong Ship) is. But Vaelthryn's presence is felt in every Undead enemy in the mountain biome.

If the player defeats the Everlong Ship at Irondeep Floor 40: they find an Accord Fragment in the wreckage, and Irondeep's barracks are fully restored. The undead tide in the mountain cities recedes.

If the player has captured and Bonded (married) an Undead Monster Girl, she may receive a "blood memory" at 200 affection — a vision of Vaelthryn's throne room, granting a hidden route to Irondeep Floor 40 that skips 10 floors of dungeon.

Hidden Discovery
The Cinderpeak Forge Secret

Cinderpeak (volcanic biome, has Blacksmith and Arcane Tower) is often skipped by players rushing to Pandemonium. But its dungeon holds a secret: at Floor 30, the Blacksmith NPC can be found trapped in a collapsed tunnel. Freeing him unlocks the ability to enhance equipment to +6 (normally capped at +5) and reveals that Valdremoch's agents have been using the volcanic forges to construct something enormous — a hint toward the Act IV superboss Ignis.

Lore Note — Monster Girls in Act III
The Forest and the Mountain Give Up Their Echoes

Veilholt's forest biome introduces Fey and Beast Monster Girls who are different — more aware, more sorrowful. They speak of "the Queen" (Sylvana) with reverence and fear. A Fey girl recruited here and taken to the House may trigger a unique dialogue at 150 affection: she sings a song in a language no one understands, and the Temple priest in Coralhaven later identifies it as a clause of the original Sundering Accord. This is how optional lore is delivered: through the House, through affection, through the quiet moments between dungeon runs.

By Act III, the player should have several Monster Girls in their House. The Rest function (which restores HP) and the Sleep function (available 20:00–04:00, advances time by 8 hours) are narrative opportunities: the girls whisper at night. The higher the house tier, the more girls can be stored, and the more lore fragments the player accumulates.

Act IV · Level Cap 40
Act IV
The Lich's Crown
Cities: Stormhold Skylume Cinderpeak Dunemar Coralhaven
Act IV Setup
The Architecture of Undeath

By Act IV the world is visibly deteriorating. Stormhold, the northern tundra fortress, reports that the glacier itself is moving — not thawing, but pressing south, as if something enormous is sleeping under it and turning over. Skylume's Arcane Tower is the only institution with records from before the Accord — records Valdremoch himself helped write. And Cinderpeak's volcanic vents are behaving in ways that suggest a Construct-type intelligence is regulating them from below.

The player is now approaching the Level Cap of 40. The Guild Hall requires 4 unique biomes cleared to Floor 40 to unlock this cap. Once achieved, the player receives a global notification: "The crystalline gates of Pandemonium shimmer in the distance." Act IV has three parallel tracks that the player can tackle in any order. All three feed into the final preparation for the Isle of Glass.

Dungeon Milestone 4–A
Stormhold's Frozen Gate
The garrison at Stormhold (shop, inn, barracks, gift_shop — no port, no trade hall) has been reduced to forty soldiers holding a fortress designed for four hundred. Giants have descended from the tundra. The player must clear the Stormhold Dungeon to Floor 40. The Superboss here is likely Broodmother Vileheart (Level 20, 350 HP) or Ignis, the Melt-Forge Golem (Level 21, 400 HP) — a towering construct of fire and metal that leaves the glacier steaming. Defeating it reveals a buried signal tower broadcasting Valdremoch's signature magic.
Reward: Stormhold restored, Accord Fragment 5 (final, optional) in the signal tower's foundation.
Facility Hook 4–B
Skylume's Archive
Skylume's Arcane Tower holds the pre-Accord Conclave records. The Tower's current master, an elderly Gnome named Tivvar, has been under mental compulsion. The player must clear the Skylume Dungeon to Floor 30 and defeat the Gnome High Artificer (Level 30 boss, Tivvar under compulsion) non-lethally — using the Capture net instead of killing blows. Tivvar, once freed, provides the Isle of Glass star-chart (key item).
Reward: Isle of Glass star-chart (key item), Tivvar freed as major NPC ally.
Dungeon Milestone 4–C
The Cinderpeak Forge
Cinderpeak's Blacksmith has discovered that the vents are being used as forges — something beneath is constructing objects of tremendous power. Descending into the Cinderpeak Dungeon to Floor 40, the player finds a construction chamber. The Superboss here is almost certainly Ignis, the Melt-Forge Golem (Level 21, 400 HP) — a construct that embodies the volcanic rage of the broken Accord. Destroying the forge chamber is possible; so is sabotaging Ignis so he malfunctions.
Reward: Titan Blueprints (key item, reveals Valdremoch's defensive plan for Act V). Epic/Legendary weapon from forge.
Facility Hook 4–D
The Last Accord Signing
Optional, unlocked by having all five Accord Fragments. Coralhaven's three Temples, when shown the fragments, initiate a Reconstruction Ceremony. The player must spend 1,000 gold and 3 days (sleeping at the inn advances time) to complete the ritual. The ceremony produces a partial translation of the original Sundering Accord. Without this, the Act V ritual against Valdremoch is harder. With it, the player gains a Legendary Scroll of Fusion and full knowledge of the phylactery's weakness.
Reward: Phylactery Ritual Knowledge, Legendary Scroll of Fusion, full Act V preparation.
Act IV Superboss — The Remaining Heralds
Rientrante and Yinglong — Reality Itself Cracks

By Act IV, the player has likely defeated 4–5 of the 7 Superbosses. The remaining two are the most bizarre and the most dangerous. Yinglong, the Heaven-Banished Dragon (Level 25, 400 HP) descends from the sky at the end of a Floor 40 dungeon run — the sky literally cracks open above the combat arena. Rientrante, the Administrator (Level 25, 420 HP) is stranger still: the dungeon flickers, the walls pixelate, and a human figure steps through the distortion saying, "You shouldn't be here." He fights with reality-warping attacks that bypass normal defense buffs.

Both are designed as the hardest non-Valdremoch encounters in the game. Yinglong requires high DEX and WIS to dodge and resist. Rientrante requires allies with learned skills that can interrupt his "Admin Override" attack (a 3-turn charge that deals 50% max HP damage). The Barracks teaching menu becomes essential here.

World State by Act IV's End
What the Player Has Changed

By the close of Act IV, the player has defeated or encountered six or seven of the seven Heralds. The Eternal Shadow (represented by Yinglong) remains partially active regardless — it is the one power that retreats rather than fights, fading when the player approaches and re-condensing elsewhere. It will play a role in the Act V sequence on the Isle of Glass.

Every city the player visited now has a changed state — restored temples, freed garrisons, reopened trade routes, or diplomatic debts depending on choices made. Tivvar from Skylume, Ssarak from Mirefall, and the Brinewatch Portmaster are all available as recurring contacts with unique final-act dialogue. If the player has Bonded (married) a Monster Girl from each of the four major biomes, she sends a letter warning the player that Valdremoch anticipated their arrival at the Isle.

The player should now have unlocked Pandemonium by clearing Floor 40 in 4 unique biomes. The Isle of Glass awaits.

Act V · Pandemonium
Act V
The Isle of Glass
Cities: Isle of Glass Tidebreak Coralhaven
Act V — The Approach
The Island That Doesn't Appear

The Isle of Glass is not on any map because it exists partially outside the mortal plane — anchored to the world by the Accord itself. With the Accord dissolved, the Isle is drifting, its anchor chain severing strand by strand. The player navigates to it using the star-chart from Skylume and a ship from Tidebreak or Coralhaven (sea routes only — Isle of Glass has no land connections). As they approach, the Isle crystallizes into visibility: a mile-long island of translucent stone, buildings grown rather than built, and at its center, a tower that spirals inward rather than upward.

The Isle of Glass has only two services: the Port and the Arcane Tower. There is no inn, no shop, no blacksmith. The Arcane Tower is not a place of rest or commerce — it is the gateway to Pandemonium, the final dungeon. When the player attempts to enter, the game checks: have they cleared Floor 40 in at least 4 unique biomes? If yes, the crystalline gates open. If no, the voice echoes: "Prove yourself across the breadth of this world before you dare tread here."

Pandemonium is a 40-floor dungeon with the pandemonium biome, which means all enemy stats are multiplied by 1.7×. The player cannot flee. The floor structure is the same (10 rooms per floor, boss every 5th floor, Superboss at Floor 20 and Floor 40), but the enemy level range is effectively 17–68 due to the multiplier. Drops are amplified: 70% drop chance, heavily skewed toward Epic and Legendary. Gold drops are 2.5× normal. The player must bring their best-equipped allies, their finest potions, and their most powerful Bonded wedding accessories.

Pandemonium Milestone 5–A
The Crystal Labyrinth
Floors 1–20 of Pandemonium. The outer Isle is a maze of mirrored crystal that reflects false paths. The Eternal Shadow manifests here — not as an enemy, but as a whisper in the non-combat rooms. "Follow the dark," it says. If the player has Bonded a Shadow Monster Girl, she can see the true path, reducing the dungeon length by 5 floors (the player can skip from Floor 15 to Floor 20). Enemies: all pandemonium races (Human, Demon, Construct, Dragonkin, Fey, Elemental, Giant, Vampire, etc.) at 1.7× stats.
Reward: Massive EXP and gold, Legendary drops, shortcut available if Shadow Bonded.
Pandemonium Milestone 5–B
The Superboss at Floor 20
At Floor 20 of Pandemonium, the player encounters the last of the seven Heralds — whichever one remains in the shuffled tier pool. In Pandemonium mode, this Superboss is 1.7× stronger than their base stats. A Broodmother with 595 HP. A Slitcurrent with 714 HP. This is the crucible. The player must use every skill, every ally, every item, and every Bonded bonus they have accumulated.
Reward: Final Superboss defeat, massive EXP bonus, unique Legendary drop.
Pandemonium Milestone 5–C
The Final Twenty Floors
Floors 21–40. The dungeon grows stranger. The non-combat rooms begin to show "memory chambers" — scenes from Valdremoch's past played out by echo-constructs. Walking through them reveals who he was before becoming a Lich: a brilliant, compassionate mage who signed the Accord in good faith, then spent decades watching it fail everyone except the four powers it constrained. The player can examine or destroy each chamber. Examining all six grants a 10% damage bonus in the final fight (mercy bonus); destroying them grants a 10% damage bonus instead (justice bonus).
Reward: Mercy bonus or Justice bonus applied to final fight.
Pandemonium Finale 5–D
The Last Lecture
At Floor 40 of Pandemonium, the player does not fight a random Superboss. They fight Arch-Lich Valdremoch — the hidden eighth boss. He has known they were coming for five acts. He wants to talk — to explain, to justify, to see if the player agrees. This is a dialogue sequence delivered through the terminal UI, reactive to major choices: number of Superbosses defeated, number of Monster Girls Bonded, number of Accord Fragments collected, and whether Tivvar or Ssarak were aided. The dialogue choices affect the final fight's conditions.
Dialogue choices affect whether NPCs from across the world send support (changing the final fight's conditions).
Arch-Lich Valdremoch
Final Boss · The Sigil-Breaker · He Who Unmade His Own Name

Valdremoch fights in two phases. Phase One: the phylactery is intact. He is functionally immortal — he can be knocked to 0 HP but will reconstitute in 2 turns at 30% max HP. Phase Two: phylactery destroyed. He is genuinely mortal for the first time in a century. He fights differently — not recklessly, but with a strange quiet, as if part of him is relieved.

His moveset is constructed from Learning-magic: delayed-detonation runes, memory-traps (WIS save or lose one turn), and a Silence field that prevents item use for two turns. His Constitution-boosted frame means he absorbs punishment well, but without the phylactery, he cannot survive. His base stats are scaled to the Pandemonium multiplier: 500+ HP, Level 30+, STR/CON/LRN all +6 or higher.

If the player has the Ritual Knowledge from Coralhaven, the phylactery can be destroyed mid-fight via a Learning check (DC 18). If the player has all five Accord Fragments, the phylactery is automatically destroyed at the start of Phase Two. If Tivvar was freed, he transmits a beacon from Skylume that helps the player resist the memory-traps for 3 turns. If Ssarak was aided, he smuggles a cache of Holy Water to the Isle — enough to break Valdremoch's silence field once.

On death, Valdremoch's body does not fall. It dissolves, leaving only the grimoire — open now, its pages blank. What the player does with the blank grimoire is the game's final choice.

Endings
What Comes After
The Final Choice — The Blank Grimoire

Valdremoch's grimoire is blank. It could be written in again. The player chooses what to do with it — and this choice, combined with the NPCs who survive and the choices made across all five acts, determines which ending plays. The choice is presented at the Isle of Glass Arcane Tower after the final combat.

A
Destroy the Grimoire — The Accord is gone; no replacement is written. The four bound powers (those who survived) are permanently free. A new age of mortal diplomacy begins, fragile and uncertain. The world is fully changed. Difficult epilogues for every city. The player's Monster Girls remain free — their Bonded status becomes permanent, but they are no longer "captured." They choose to stay.
B
Write a New Accord — Using everything the player learned, they draft a new treaty with the surviving powers. Requires WIS 18+ or Tivvar's assistance. The new Accord is imperfect but honest. A hard-won stability. Cities with strong ally relationships have better outcomes. The Monster Girls become the new Witnesses — their Bonded wedding accessories are the signatures of the new treaty.
C
Claim the Grimoire — The player keeps it. The blank pages can record whatever they choose. Power, knowledge, a weapon, a new name. This ending's epilogue is uniquely tied to the player's class and race — each combination produces different text. Morally ambiguous; the game does not judge. The Monster Girls' reactions vary by affection: some are frightened, some are proud, one may ask to leave.
D
Return it to the Eternal Shadow — The one power never confronted directly. Giving it the grimoire is an act of trust in something unknowable. The Shadow takes it and disappears. Whether this was wise, no one can say. Unlocks a secret post-credits scene. Requires the player to have Bonded a Shadow Monster Girl and reached 200 affection with her.

Design Notes
Alignment with TerminalRPG Systems
System Integration
How the Story Uses Existing Mechanics

Level Cap System. The story is explicitly mapped to the level cap progression: 10 → 20 → 30 → 40 → Pandemonium. Each act requires the player to visit the Guild Hall and ascend their cap, which in turn requires clearing unique biomes to deeper floors. This creates natural narrative beats: the player cannot rush to the endgame; they must explore the world.

Superboss Tier Pool. The 7 existing superbosses (Broodmother, Slitcurrent, Sylvana, Ignis, Yinglong, Rientrante, Everlong Ship) are the Heralds of the Four Bound Powers. They appear at Floor 20 and Floor 40 of every city dungeon, drawn from a shuffled pool. The story explains why they exist: they are echoes of the Accord's breaking. No new superbosses need to be coded for the main story; the existing seven are sufficient.

Valdremoch as the 8th Boss. The final boss is not a random dungeon enemy. He is a hidden superboss who only appears at Floor 40 of Pandemonium (Isle of Glass), replacing the normal Superboss encounter. His module would be combat/valdremoch.py, imported in dungeon.py, and added to a special "final boss" tier that overrides the pool when pandemonium_mode == True and floor == 40.

Monster Girl Integration. The capture and House systems are central to the lore. Monster Girls are "Echoes" of the broken Accord. The House is a sanctuary. The Wedding / Engagement system (100 affection → ring from Gift Shop → 200 affection → Legendary wedding accessory) represents forming a "Bond" — a tiny, personal re-weaving of the Accord. The wedding accessories' Bonded combat effects are mechanically justified by the story: they are clauses of the new treaty.

Facility-Based Story Delivery. The narrative is delivered through existing facilities rather than custom quest engines: Trade Hall for rumors and maps, Black Market for illicit information, Temple for blessings and lore, Arcane Tower for star-charts and records, Barracks for ally training and military quests, Herbalist for plague cures, Port/Shipyard for sea travel, Gift Shop for engagement rings, and Guild Hall for level cap ascension and biome tracking.

Dungeon Milestones. "Missions" are not custom quests; they are dungeon floor objectives. "Clear Floor 20" or "Defeat the Floor 40 Superboss" or "Find the hidden room at Floor 30." The existing 10-room floor system, boss rooms, and non-combat rooms (fountain, merchant, trap, treasure, stat-check) are the story's stage. The player does not need to be told to "hold the walls" — they experience the rising threat through harder enemies, undead races appearing in new biomes, and Superbosses at the milestone floors.

Travel Events. The road between cities is not safe. The travel system (land and sea) already has random events. The Amber Road Ambush (Act III) is a travel event that spawns a Dragonkin boss. The pirate assault on the way to Blackwake (Act II) is a sea travel event. These require no new code — just new dialogue entries in the travel event tables.

Save Compatibility. The story is delivered through existing systems (dialogue, dungeon drops, travel events). It does not require new persistent fields beyond possibly a few flags (e.g., "orvast_journal_found", "accord_fragments" list). These can be added with .setdefault() in ensure_player_fields() to maintain backward compatibility with old saves.

Pandemonium Dungeon. The Isle of Glass already is the Pandemonium Dungeon. The story does not need to describe a separate tower. The 40-floor dungeon with 1.7× stats, the pandemonium biome, and the unlock condition (40 floors in 4 biomes) are all already implemented. The story simply gives them narrative meaning.

Dialogue Limitations. The game is a terminal text RPG. There are no cutscenes, no branching dialogue trees, no cinematic camera angles. The "dialogue" with Valdremoch is a text sequence printed before the final combat, with 2–3 choices that set flags affecting the fight. The player's "choices" across the story are their gameplay actions: which cities they cleared, which Superbosses they defeated, which Monster Girls they Bonded, which NPCs they aided. These are tracked automatically by the existing game state.

The Blank Grimoire

At the end, when the Lich has dissolved and the tower is quiet and the sea outside is still for the first time in months, the player holds a book with nothing written in it.

The Sundering Accord was a document of obligation. Whatever comes next — if anything comes next — will be a document of choice.

That is the difference. And it is everything.
